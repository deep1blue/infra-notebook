Reward Shaping for Agentic Models: The Industry Field Guide

Fifteen shaping techniques actually used in production post-training — ordered by strength of public evidence, each with the distortion it buys you.

SFT and RL Lesson

Builds on: the evening RL Q&A — PPO, DPO, GRPO, RLVR — and tonight's 5pm drill on the λ length penalty, which was already shaping in disguise. This post is the reference doc for all of it.

An honest note on ordering: there is no universal efficacy ranking for shaping techniques — what works best depends on task, domain, and what you measure. The tiers below are ordered by strength of public evidence plus breadth of industry adoption, the closest honest proxy. Where a lab has publicly disclosed or credibly reported usage, it's named; where details are proprietary, that's stated outright.

The master equation

Every entry below is a term in one sum:

rshaped(x, y) = rtask(x, y) + Σi si(x, y)

The founding result, and the only free lunch in the field: potential-based shaping (Ng, Harada & Russell, 1999) — adding γΦ(s′) − Φ(s) — is the only shaping guaranteed not to change the optimal policy. Every other term below deliberately redefines what "good" means. That redefinition is the tool and the hazard.

Tier 1 — Industry standard

Disclosed by multiple labs, load-bearing in production post-training stacks.

1. KL anchor r − β·KL(π ‖ πref)

Buys: keeps RL from destroying the SFT model — the policy can improve without drifting into gibberish. Shaping as a leash.

Used by: OpenAI's InstructGPT (2022); now universal across RLHF stacks.

Distorts: timidity. Too-high β and the policy never explores far enough from SFT for RL to matter; too-low β and the anchor is decorative.

2. Verifiable outcome rewards (RLVR) r = 1[y correct]

Buys: replaces a hackable learned reward with binary, rule-based correctness on math/code. Kills reward hacking by construction — where correctness is checkable.

Used by: DeepSeek-R1 (2025), paired with format rewards; the defining move of the reasoning-model era.

Distorts: confined to verifiable domains; models still hack the verifier (exploiting weak tests, gaming exact-match).

3. Length penalty r − λ·tokens

Buys: prices every generated token — counters RL's tendency to reward-hack via verbosity ("overthinking"). Training-time version of the inference bill.

Used by: Meta's "thought compression" for Muse Spark (described in Meta's 2026 announcements); the efficient-reasoning literature broadly.

Distorts: underthinking and truncation — or degenerate near-silence if applied without gating on correctness. Gate it: penalize length only among correct answers, or hinge past a budget.

4. Format rewards +b if structure matches

Buys: structural compliance — thinking tags, answer delimiters, tool-call syntax. Cheap, verifiable, and surprisingly load-bearing for downstream parsing.

Used by: DeepSeek-R1 (format + accuracy rewards).

Distorts: well-formatted nonsense — the model learns the shape of compliance with none of the substance.

Tier 2 — Frontier-adopted

Strong paper evidence, real deployment at frontier labs.

5. Process rewards (PRMs) — dense step-level signal

Buys: densifies a sparse outcome reward into per-step feedback. The classic shaping motivation: sparse rewards are brutal to learn from.

Used by: OpenAI, "Let's Verify Step by Step" (Lightman et al., 2023).

Distorts: step labels are expensive; the PRM itself gets hacked step-by-step; credit assignment stays hard.

6. Rejection sampling on trajectories — filter, then train

Buys: only the best sampled trajectories become training data. Simple, stable, no value network, no KL tuning wars.

Used by: industry-wide; Meta's Muse Spark 1.2 co-training on rejection-sampled harness trajectories (reported 2026).

Distorts: inherits the filter's biases wholesale; purely offline — zero exploration beyond what the sampler already produces.

7. DPO implicit reward β·log(π/πref)

Buys: the reward model never gets built — preferences optimize the policy directly through the closed-form KL-constrained optimum. The 2023 simplification that ate RLHF's lunch for offline tuning.

Used by: Rafailov et al., 2023; the DPO/KTO/SimPO/ORPO family is industry-standard offline preference tuning.

Distorts: length bias (longer reads as better); offline distribution shift; the R-DPO/SimPO patches exist precisely because of these distortions.

8. Multi-objective scalarization Σ wk·rk

Buys: helpfulness, harmlessness, honesty (etc.) combined into one trainable scalar. Every production assistant is a compromise; this is the compromise written down.

Used by: Anthropic's assistant training; Constitutional AI lineage (Bai et al., 2022).

Distorts: the weights are arbitrary trades, and optimizing a weighted sum Goodharts each component — the policy games whichever objective is cheapest to satisfy.

9. RLAIF / AI-judge rewards

Buys: preference labels at machine scale — a strong model judges outputs instead of humans. Unblocks data volume that human labeling can't reach.

Used by: Bai et al., 2022; AI judges now routine across labs.

Distorts: the judge's biases become the model's biases — the same disease as LLM-filtered SFT data, one stage later.

Tier 3 — Real usage, narrower evidence

Deployed or credibly reported; thinner public detail.

10. Self-rewarding LMs — the model judges itself

Buys: iterative self-improvement: the model generates, judges, and trains on its own outputs, bootstrapping past its training data.

Used by: Meta (Yuan et al., 2024).

Distorts: judge and policy co-adapt into self-congratulation loops; needs held-out grounding or the model just learns to flatter itself.

11. Uncertainty-penalized rewards — distrust what the RM is unsure about

Buys: penalizes regions where the reward model has high variance or ensemble members disagree — a direct anti-hacking defense.

Used by: various labs' anti-hacking stacks; exact production recipes rarely disclosed.

Distorts: over-conservatism — the policy avoids anything novel; ensembles are expensive at RL scale.

12. Tool-trajectory rewards — reward intermediate tool success

Buys: in agentic rollouts, intermediate tool calls get their own reward signal instead of one sparse terminal reward. Densification for the agentic setting.

Used by: coding-agent training; reported in harness co-training setups.

Distorts: rewards tool use, not task success — agents learn to call tools theatrically without advancing the goal.

13. Budget / hinge shaping −λ·max(0, tokens − budget)

Buys: the length penalty only bites past a budget — free reasoning up to the allowance, priced after. L1-style variants control length nearly exactly.

Used by: efficient-reasoning research; some production inference-budget controls.

Distorts: cliff effects at the budget boundary; genuinely hard problems get starved exactly when they need tokens most.

Tier 4 — Theory and frontier

Know these for depth; rarely the thing that ships.

14. Potential-based shaping γΦ(s′) − Φ(s)

Buys: the only shaping with a mathematical guarantee of preserving the optimal policy. The reference point every other term is judged against.

Used by: Ng, Harada & Russell (1999); honored more in citation than in production LLM code.

Distorts: nothing, by construction — which is why it's here and not in Tier 1. Designing good potentials for language is genuinely hard.

15. Exploration bonuses — curiosity

Buys: rewards novelty to force exploration in sparse-reward settings.

Used by: robotics and game RL; rare in LLM post-training, where SFT already provides broad coverage.

Distorts: noisy, sample-inefficient; the policy explores curiosities instead of the task.

The Staff checklist

How senior engineers actually wield this list:

  1. Gate every penalty on task success — correctness-gating or hinges. An ungated penalty always has a degenerate optimum (silence, timidity, format without content).
  2. Add shaping terms one at a time and ablate. Two new terms interacting is how you spend a month debugging a reward function instead of a model.
  3. Watch the distortion metrics, not just the reward curve. Track response length, KL distance from πref, format compliance, and task accuracy separately. The reward going up while length collapses is a failure wearing a success costume.
  4. Write down what you traded. Shaping redefines "good" — document each term's intended distortion so the next engineer (or your future self) knows which behaviors were bought, not discovered.
30-second interview answer

"Reward shaping modifies the reward to make learning tractable — the KL anchor, length penalties, verifiable rewards, process rewards, format bonuses. Only potential-based shaping preserves the optimal policy; everything else deliberately redefines 'good,' so each term buys a distortion: length penalties buy underthinking, the KL anchor buys timidity, format rewards buy well-formatted nonsense. The Staff skill is gating penalties on correctness and watching the distortion metrics, not just the reward curve."

Connects to

B3 — RLHF pipeline: this is where every term above gets wired into PPO/GRPO/RLOO — the KL anchor and length penalty live inside the training loop, not beside it. B4 — reward modeling: the reward being shaped has to be built first; Bradley–Terry on dirty preference pairs is the same disease one stage earlier. E2 — eval harnesses: decontamination and held-out evals are how you catch the distortions these terms introduce.

SFT gotchas

The eight ways fine-tuning quietly goes wrong — follow-up to the SFT lesson.

SFT and RL Q&A

Follow-up to: SFT: Teaching the Model Its Manners — the questions that came right after the lesson, answered.

1.Template mismatch

Train with one chat template, serve with another (or none), and the model silently degrades. It only ever learned the exact token format it trained on. This wastes more weeks than any other SFT bug.

2.Forgetting loss masking

If loss runs on prompt tokens too, you're training the model to predict user instructions — literally teaching it to roleplay the human instead of answering. Mask every prompt token with −100.

3.Masking the wrong turns

In multi-turn data, every assistant turn should count; every user/system turn shouldn't. One off-by-one in your masking code and you're training on the wrong half of the conversation.

4.Too much training

More than ~1–3 epochs or too high a learning rate and you start eroding pretraining knowledge (catastrophic forgetting). SFT is nudging settled weights, not rebuilding them — keep the LR small, roughly 1–2e−5.

5.Junk data at scale

LIMA's lesson cuts both ways: 1k excellent examples beat 1M mediocre ones. Scraped instruction data full of wrong answers teaches confident wrongness. Quality dominates quantity.

6.Contamination

Benchmark questions leaking into your SFT data makes eval scores lie to you. Decontaminate, or your "gains" are fiction.

7.Packing without boundaries

Concatenating short examples into long sequences for efficiency is standard — but without proper attention masking, the model cross-attends between unrelated examples and learns spurious links.

8.Style overfitting

SFT rewards the shape of good answers, so models learn to sound helpful — verbose, sycophantic — without being right. That's exactly what the RL stage exists to fix, and it's the next node on the knowledge tree.
